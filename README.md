# Police-Killings-in-America-Data-Inequality-Patterns-Since-2015


Since January 1st, 2015, **The Washington Post** has been maintaining the most comprehensive, publicly available database of fatal police shootings in the United States.  
This project analyzes that dataset and supplements it with **U.S. Census socioeconomic indicators** to better understand patterns, disparities, and structural inequality.

---

## 📌 Project Goals

✅ Explore demographic patterns in fatal police shootings  
✅ Identify which cities and states experience the most killings  
✅ Examine relationships between police violence and:
- poverty rates  
- educational attainment  
- household income  
- racial composition  

✅ Analyze trends over time  
✅ Visualize weapon use, age distributions, geographic patterns, and racial disparities

---

## 📂 Datasets Used

### 1. **Fatal Police Shootings — The Washington Post**
Contains:
- date of killing  
- race, age, gender of deceased  
- armed/unarmed status + weapon type  
- location (city, county, state)  
- mental health indicators  
- policing circumstances  
- sources and case reporting notes

Collected from:
- law enforcement reports
- local news coverage
- public records
- independent databases (e.g., *Fatal Encounters*, *Killed by Police*)

---

### 2. **U.S. Census Data**
Used for socioeconomic context:

- poverty rate by state
- high school graduation rate
- median household income
- racial population demographics

Source: U.S. Census Bureau

---

## 🔍 Key Questions

- Which states and cities have the most fatal police encounters?
- Are certain racial groups disproportionately affected?
- Does poverty correlate with police killings?
- Do high-poverty states show higher per-capita fatality rates?
- Are victims typically armed, and if so — with what?
- How have fatal police shootings changed over time since 2015?

## 📊 Visualizations Included
